The problem Supersoft is trying to solve is, for example, if I'm a member of a non-profit,
and I use the non-profit's software, and I find it a little difficult to sign up,
or difficult to join an event, or to find content.
Everything can be done, but it's a little complicated, a little odd.
It's not intuitive, it's not clear, it's not smooth.
And as a leader, when I ask the developers to change things,
communication is a little difficult, everything is very expensive,
we change something, but it creates other problems elsewhere, etc.
Nothing is very simple.
To solve that, the solution is for the developers to talk and understand.
And for them to understand perfectly the need, the business, the non-profit's domain.
And for you, as a leader, to understand perfectly the developers' craft too,
and what they need to understand well, and to make software that is easy to use,
maintainable, easy to evolve, etc.
And even, why not...
Why not take part yourself in building the software.
And thanks to that, you get something that is very smooth, very intuitive for users,
and if users want to change something,
it's very simple to propose changes to the software,
it's smooth, really.
That both using the software is smooth,
and the way it evolves is smooth.
The two go together.
That's the idea.
And so, Supersoft is a platform for exchange
between developers and the leaders of a non-profit,
the people who commission the software,
exchanging to clarify the needs,
plan the changes carefully,
test, check that it really matches,
get it validated by users, etc.

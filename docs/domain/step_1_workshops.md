# Workshops

Previous: the [scope](project.md) of the project.

The domain experts explain each part of the business, each domain, in workshops. What was said and shown is recorded as it came, then transcribed. The aim is to capture the domain experts' natural language and their natural mental model of the business, even when it is not formalised yet and holds ambiguities and holes.

At the start of a project, before any domain is written, the maker can make a sketch of the whole scope and show it in a workshop: something rough the domain experts can react to, which brings out the parts of the business and the words they use for them.

Next: the [domains](step_2_domains.md) are written from the workshops.

## Risks

The domain experts talk about the interface they want, not about the business they do. A workshop asks what they do, and why; the screens come later, from the prototype. A sketch taken for a prototype makes this worse: it is there to bring out the business, not to settle a screen.

## Glossary

- **Workshop**: one working session with the domain experts, and the documents produced in it.
- **Date of a workshop**: the day it was held.
- **Title of a workshop**: what it was about.
- **Workshop document**: a document produced in a workshop. It says what kind it is — slides, a video, a recording, notes, a report, a transcript — and where it is, so that anyone reading the project can go back to it.
- **Sketch**: a rough model of the whole scope, as the domain experts can try it, made before the domains are written. It is a workshop document.
- **Text of a document**: what it says, when it is written directly in the project — notes, a report — and is then part of the project.

## Rules

**Rule.** A workshop is dated. It is not a source of truth: it is what was said on one day. What was understood later is a later workshop, or it is written in a domain, where the domain expert can contradict it.

**Rule.** A document can be added to a workshop after the day it was held — a report or a transcript often comes later — and it is always about that day.

**Rule.** Workshops are read by date, the most recent first.

**Rule.** A sketch covers the whole scope, roughly, and depends on nothing outside itself.

**Rule.** A sketch is never updated and never validated. Like the rest of its workshop, it is what was understood on one day: what was understood later is written in the domains, and shown in a prototype. A prototype may start from the screens of a sketch; the sketch stays as it was.

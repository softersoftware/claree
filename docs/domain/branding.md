# Branding

How a digital tool presents itself: its name, how it speaks, how it looks. Every project has one. Branding belongs to no step of the cycle: it is chosen once, early, and every final interface applies it.

It is chosen once the scope is clear enough :

1. **Discovery** — in a workshop, the domain experts and the project owner say who will use the digital tool, what else those people use, and what the tool must never be mistaken for.
2. **Personality** — in the same workshop, they agree on a few words that say what the digital tool should feel like, before anything is drawn.
3. **Naming** — when the digital tool has no name yet, the maker suggests some, and checks that each is free to use and means nothing unwanted in the languages of its users.
4. **Tone** — the maker writes how the digital tool speaks, and its tagline.
5. **Directions** — the maker, a UI designer or a UX designer, suggests a few directions, each shown in a branding prototype. The project owner chooses one, and the maker refines it into the colours, the typography, the logo, the iconography, the shapes and the density.
6. **Trying it** — the chosen direction is shown on a few screens of the prototype, to see that it holds there. The project owner agrees to it.
7. **Keeping it** — the branding agreed is kept in the project's repository, and every final interface takes it from there.
8. **Changing it** — only the project owner changes the branding, by agreeing to a new one. A small change refines it; a new personality starts again from the workshop.

## Risks

Choosing it too early, for a digital tool nobody has seen yet. Choosing colours people like rather than a personality the tool needs: the workshop asks what the tool should feel like, and the look comes later, from the maker. Applying it too early, to prototypes that are still there to change.

## Glossary

- **Branding**: how a digital tool presents itself. It holds its name, its tagline, its tone, its colours, its typography, its logo, its iconography, its shapes and its density.
- **Personality**: a few words that say what the digital tool should feel like. Every other part of the branding answers to it.
- **Name**: what the digital tool is called.
- **Tagline**: one line saying what the digital tool is.
- **Tone**: how the digital tool speaks, in its interface and in its documents.
- **Visible string**: any text the digital tool shows.
- **Colours**: the colours of the digital tool, and what each one is for.
- **Typography**: the typefaces, and how text is set.
- **Logo**: the mark of the digital tool, and the forms it takes: the icon of the application, the icon of a browser tab.
- **Iconography**: the style of the icons and of the illustrations.
- **Shapes and density**: the corners, the spacing, and how much fits on a screen.
- **Direction**: one possible look for the digital tool, suggested by the maker before the project owner chooses.
- **Branding prototype**: a few screens made to show a direction.

## Rules

**Rule.** A project has one branding at a time, and keeps it in its repository.

**Rule.** The branding is chosen after the scope, once the scope is clear enough to say what the digital tool is.

**Rule.** The personality is agreed in a workshop before any direction is suggested.

**Rule.** A name is checked before it is suggested: free to use, and meaning nothing unwanted in the languages of the digital tool's users.

**Rule.** The maker suggests more than one direction; the project owner chooses.

**Rule.** The project owner agrees to the branding, as to anything: something named, on a date. Only a new agreement changes it.

**Rule.** A branding prototype, like any prototype, depends on nothing outside itself.

**Rule.** Sketches and prototypes may ignore the branding: they try the business and the journeys, not the look.

**Rule.** A mock-up applies the branding: the final interface is the branded one.

**Rule.** Every visible string answers to the tone.
